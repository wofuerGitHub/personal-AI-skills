# Examples — LinkedIn Communication

These examples demonstrate the expected behavior of `skill.communication.linkedin-communication`.

They are illustrative rather than templates to copy mechanically.

## 1. Future-oriented technical post

### Input

- Picture: engineers reviewing a test setup
- Principle: trust
- Topic: validation
- Future emphasis: preparing for more complex products

### Expected behavior

- Open with a concrete validation observation.
- Use one or two supported mechanisms such as simulation, testing, tolerances, interfaces, or load paths.
- Connect trust to evidence.
- Broaden from component-level work to larger system complexity.
- Do not make ECC the default narrative center.
- Do not invent measured results.
- End with a practical, domain-relevant question or restrained statement.

### Illustrative output

Trust in engineering is built before a product reaches the customer.

Validation is where assumptions meet evidence. Simulation can help us understand loads and critical interfaces early, while physical testing shows where the real system behaves differently from the model.

The important capability is not one test or one tool. It is the discipline to connect design, simulation, and validation so that decisions become more reliable.

As products combine more software, electronics, mechanics, and data, that discipline has to scale with the complexity of the system.

What validation capability becomes more important as your products become more interconnected?

## 3. Technical explainer: thermal management

### Input

- Picture: finned electronic housing
- Principle: reliability
- Topic: thermal management
- Verified context: no measured temperature result supplied

### Expected behavior

- Explain a real heat-transfer or thermal-design mechanism.
- Connect design, simulation, and validation to reliability.
- Do not invent a temperature reduction or performance percentage.

### Illustrative output

Thermal management is often decided by small design details.

A housing, cooling fins, interface material, and the contact path between components all influence how effectively heat can move away from sensitive electronics. Simulation helps us compare concepts early, but validation remains essential because interfaces and material behavior are never perfectly ideal.

For me, this is a good example of reliability by design: understand the mechanism, model it, test it, and improve the weak points.

As electronics become more compact and powerful, this capability becomes even more important across the complete system.

## 4. Cross-domain future

### Input

- Picture: cross-functional engineering meeting
- Principle: collaboration
- Topic: software/electronics/mechanical integration
- Future emphasis: increasingly complex products

### Expected behavior

- Make the interfaces between domains the subject.
- Avoid saying collaboration is important without explaining why.
- Connect cross-domain work to larger product/system complexity.

### Illustrative output

Many engineering problems no longer belong to one discipline.

A mechanical decision can influence electronics, thermal behavior, sensors, software functions, and ultimately validation. The same is true in the other direction: software requirements increasingly shape hardware and physical-system decisions.

The capability we need is therefore not only deep expertise in each domain. It is the ability to understand the interfaces well enough to make good system decisions together.

That becomes more important as products grow in complexity and development cycles remain demanding.

#SystemsEngineering #Collaboration #Software #Electronics #MechanicalEngineering

## 5. Learning as applied capability

### Input

- Context: completed an AI/data course
- Principle: continuous learning
- Topic: machine learning on engineering data
- Verified result: course completed
- No production deployment result supplied

### Expected behavior

- Mention the learning milestone.
- Connect it to a real engineering problem.
- Do not imply production deployment, savings, or business impact.
- Treat learning as stronger problem-solving capability, not credential collecting.

### Illustrative output

A course is useful when it changes how we approach a real problem.

I recently completed further learning in machine learning and data analysis. For engineering, the interesting part is not the certificate itself. It is how methods can help us work with requirements, validation, production, or simulation data while still applying domain knowledge and checking data quality.

The tools will continue to change. Building the ability to connect engineering judgment with data methods is the more durable capability.

## 10. Unsupported metric must be omitted

### Input

- Picture: prototype test
- Principle: quality
- Topic: validation
- User statement: “The test went well.”
- No measured result supplied

### Bad output

> Our new validation method improved reliability by 30% and reduced development time by 20%.

### Correct behavior

Use conservative wording such as:

> The test gave us useful evidence for the next design decisions.

Do not turn a qualitative statement into a quantitative result.

## 12. Anti-hype rewrite

### Input

- Picture: team workshop
- Principle: readiness
- Topic: operations
- Requested idea: future readiness

### Avoid

> We are revolutionizing the future with a game-changing operating model.

### Prefer

> Better operating routines help teams make decisions faster and handle more complexity without losing clarity.

The preferred version keeps the future signal but ties it to an actual capability.
