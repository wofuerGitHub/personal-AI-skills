# LinkedIn Communication

A source-grounded LinkedIn writing skill for Wolfgang Fürst.

The skill turns concrete engineering, operational, learning, people, event, and technology context into concise LinkedIn communication while preserving a direct, technical, pragmatic writing fingerprint and deliberately broadening the narrative toward cross-domain capability, scalable execution, and readiness for larger future challenges.

## What the skill helps with

The skill supports:

- original LinkedIn posts;
- technical explainers;
- principle-led engineering and operations posts;
- future-oriented capability posts;
- AI/ML, data, simulation, manufacturing, validation, and cross-domain engineering topics;
- learning and resource recommendations;
- event and milestone retrospectives;
- people recognition based on concrete contributions;
- short captions for shared posts or articles;
- current-work-to-future-capability positioning.

It does **not** define a style for private LinkedIn messages, sales outreach, recruiting outreach, or comment-thread behavior because the supplied source material does not establish those patterns.

## Core positioning

The preferred progression is:

**what we are doing or seeing now → what capability this builds → what larger future challenge it prepares us for**

The aim is not to turn technical posts into generic executive branding. The progression should remain credible:

**technical depth → cross-domain capability → scalable execution → people/system capability → readiness for larger complexity**

Future orientation should be tied to capabilities such as:

- planning and strategy-to-execution;
- reliability and evidence-based validation;
- cross-domain collaboration;
- decision quality;
- AI/data/automation as operating capabilities;
- handling complexity and uncertainty;
- systems and teams that scale;
- broader operational readiness.

Do not invent a future role, promotion, title, business result, or responsibility that the user has not supplied.

## Style fingerprint

Preferred characteristics:

- direct professional English;
- technically grounded rather than generic;
- concrete engineering or operational nouns;
- short-to-medium paragraphs;
- practical explanations;
- simple contrasts;
- moderate optimism;
- selective rhetorical questions;
- `we` for shared capability;
- appreciative but evidence-based people recognition;
- cross-domain framing;
- restrained close;
- 3–7 relevant hashtags for normal original posts.

Avoid:

- influencer language;
- exaggerated claims;
- generic leadership slogans;
- unsupported metrics;
- vague “innovation” language without a concrete mechanism or implication;
- default centering on Bosch Engineering Center Cluj (ECC);
- close copying of historical posts.

## Source policy

When learning or applying the Wolfgang writing fingerprint:

1. Full Wolfgang-authored posts are the strongest evidence.
2. Wolfgang-authored captions added to shared posts are secondary evidence.
3. Minimal/link-only authored captions are low-weight evidence.
4. Bare reposts with no Wolfgang-authored text are excluded.
5. Embedded third-party text in a shared post is not Wolfgang-authored style evidence.
6. Same-name profiles, comments, or unattributed text must not be treated as source evidence.

Historical ECC frequency is not a target for future writing. ECC may be mentioned when factually relevant, but the conclusion should normally broaden to capability, execution, or future complexity.

## Best inputs

Minimum useful inputs:

1. a picture or concrete situation;
2. a principle or message to communicate;
3. a topic to emphasize.

Helpful optional inputs:

- what happened;
- why it matters;
- verified result;
- event/project/team context;
- names and roles;
- target audience;
- future capability being built;
- larger challenge ahead;
- preferred CTA;
- required or forbidden hashtags.

## Output modes

### Original post

Usually 70–180 words with:

- a concrete opening;
- one clear principle;
- real technical, operational, or leadership substance;
- 1–3 supported mechanisms or terms when useful;
- a future-facing implication when natural;
- a restrained close;
- normally 3–7 hashtags.

### Share caption

Usually 1–3 sentences:

- explain why the shared item matters;
- add one distinct cross-domain, practical, or future-capability perspective;
- do not summarize or rewrite the shared article.

### People recognition

Focus on:

- milestone or context;
- concrete contribution;
- team/product/capability impact that is actually supplied;
- thanks or congratulations.

Do not infer personality traits, performance ratings, or business impact.

## Typical archetypes

- Technical explainer
- Principle-led engineering
- Technology → capability → future
- Learning / capability-building
- Cross-domain future
- People / contribution
- Event / retrospective
- Short endorsement / share caption
- Book / resource recommendation

## Example request

> Picture: engineers in a cross-functional planning workshop  
> Principle: planning  
> Topic: AI-enabled operations  
> Future angle: preparing teams for larger and more complex operational tasks

A strong result should connect the visible/current context to a practical capability, show why that capability matters as technology changes, and end with a grounded future implication rather than a generic leadership claim.
